Tricontinental Exchange in Business and Leadership Education

Fifteen people, three campuses, one year.

This is the system for the TREBLE exchange site. A map and a timeline where each scholar leaves a trail, and the trails cross. It is built on the official mark, the three school blues and the decisions already made in the build file and the logo spec.

01 Overview

Positioning

TREBLE is a small, selective programme run by three serious schools. The site is where the scholars keep the record of the year for themselves, for each other and for the people at home. It should feel like a well kept notebook from someone who will end up at a good firm, not like a recruitment brochure.

Nordic business casual meets American minimalism. That means warm paper neutrals and deep navy ink from the Nordic side, generous space and plain grotesque type from the American side, and the three school blues used sparingly as ink. Quiet and sure of itself. Never smug, never cute.

What it is

A shared logbook of one year across Chapel Hill, Singapore and Copenhagen, kept by the people living it.

Who reads it

The scholars first. Then parents, friends and future applicants. Later, possibly, recruiters.

How it should feel

Like a navy blazer with an oxford shirt. Correct without trying. Personal in the details.

Tone of voice

Write like a postcard to a smart friend. Say the place, the date and the thing. Short sentences, first person, no exclamation marks. Numbers do the bragging, adjectives do not. In Danish, always use du, keep English names as they are, and write dates the Danish way.

Do and do not

Do

  • Lead with the place and the date. Chapel Hill, 12 September.
  • Let the map and the photos carry the emotion.
  • Use the school blues as ink on neutral ground.
  • Use the three bar stripe to connect things. Dividers, loading, the timeline.
  • Keep the TREBLE mark small and in maker positions. Nav, footer, favicon.
  • Write both languages properly. Danish is not a translation afterthought.

Do not

  • Say journey, unforgettable, amazing, world class or global leaders of tomorrow.
  • Use emoji, exclamation marks or flags as language buttons.
  • Fill big areas with Carolina Blue. It turns the site into a bookstore.
  • Put school names on the landmark graphics. They stay anonymous.
  • Use stock photos, stickers, collages or heavy filters.
  • Make it look like the university wrote it. It is ours, not theirs.

Things I flagged instead of changing

Your earlier decisions stand. Where two of them collide, or where a decision does not work on screen, it is listed here with what the kit does in the meantime.

Carolina Blue fails as text on light

It measures 2.68 to 1 on paper and needs 4.5. The wordmark is a logo, so it is exempt, but on the site Carolina Blue cannot be body text, links or a focus ring on light. CBS Blue takes the link role in light mode. Carolina Blue becomes the link colour in dark mode, where it passes at 6.05.

No compliant full colour version on dark

The navy bar vanishes on navy or charcoal (1.14 to 1). The spec asks for a full colour on dark variant, but the only way to make one is to recolour a bar, which the misuse rules forbid. Dark mode uses the single colour paper version until the programme office supplies an official reversed file.

The Treble II '26 badge and the circle rule

The logo spec says never place the lockup inside a badge or circle. If your star ring badge uses the TREBLE lockup, it breaks that rule. The kit keeps the badge out of the site and sets the cohort as plain text, TREBLE II '26. Decide whether the badge is a separate cohort mark or gets reworked.

Two small numbers that disagree

The spec gives the mark as 108 units wide, but its own bar and gap widths add to 111. The kit keeps the 1.8 to 1 bar to gap ratio, so the mark is 111. The spec also calls the wordmark light, while a 13 percent stem matches a regular weight. The stand in uses Regular for that reason.

02 Logo system

TREBLE is the label. It does not shout.

The mark is three bars in CBS Blue, Carolina Blue and SMU Blue, in that order. The middle bar sits higher than the others and has a flat foot. The outer bars are cut at 21 degrees. That raised middle bar is the detail people lose when they redraw it, so always use the files.

Stand in wordmark. The real typeface was never identified. These files use Jost Regular (free, SIL Open Font License) outlined to paths and spaced to the measured 0.443 cap height letter gap. It is close in construction, it is not the original. Replace the wordmark paths once the programme office confirms the face.

04 Typography

A Norwegian grotesque, a reading serif, a mono for facts.

Schibsted Grotesk was drawn for a Norwegian media group, which gives the Nordic side of the brief a real root. 07 Map styling

A pale base, so the people stand out.

The base map is paper and steel in light mode and deep ink in dark. Roads and labels sit back. Colour on the map belongs to the scholars and to nothing else. The campuses are the only fixed landmarks and the only place the school graphics appear.

Base map

  • MapLibre GL with a Protomaps basemap served from Cloudflare R2. No API keys, no per view billing, fits the free tier.
  • Land --map-land, water --map-water, parks barely green, buildings one step darker than land.
  • Labels in Schibsted Grotesk 400 at 11 to 13 px, colour --map-label with a 1.5 px halo in land colour.
  • Hide POI icons, transit icons and road shields. Keep country, city and neighbourhood names.
  • No 3D buildings, no terrain, no satellite. The map is a sheet of paper.

Trails and crossings

  • Ground movement is a solid line 2.5 px wide at city zoom, round caps and joins, with a 1 px casing in the background colour.
  • Flights are great circle arcs at 1.5 px and 70 percent opacity, so long jumps never dominate.
  • Seats 9 to 16 use the same hues with a dash of 2 by 1.4 line widths.
  • Planned or future segments drop to 35 percent. When one scholar is focused, everyone else turns --map-dimmed.
  • A crossing is two or more scholars within 2 km on the same local day. Consecutive days in one place merge into one crossing.
  • Campuses are square tiles with the landmark glyph and a city label. Never the school name on the glyph.
08 Motion

Move like a person walking, not like an app showing off.

Motion explains where things came from and where they went. The map flies, trails draw, panels slide. Everything else changes quietly or not at all.

Never animate

  • The logo. No assembling bars, no reveals, no spinning mark as a loader.
  • The language switch. Text swaps instantly, layout must not jump.
  • Scholar colours. A person's colour never fades, pulses or cycles.
  • The map on load. No auto tour, no rotation, no camera move the visitor did not ask for.
  • Scrolling. No parallax, no scroll jacking, no counters ticking up.
  • Anything private. A followers only upload appears without flourish.

With reduced motion on, durations drop to zero, fly to becomes jump to, trails appear whole and the timeline play steps day by day.

09 Imagery

Honest photos from fifteen different phones.

The photos will come from iPhones, Pixels and whatever else people carry. Consistency comes from processing and framing, not from a filter on top.

On upload, automatically

  • Convert HEIC and Display P3 to sRGB, then serve AVIF and WebP through Cloudflare Images.
  • Rotate by EXIF, then strip all EXIF from the served file. Read the date and location first and store them server side. A followers only photo must never leak its location in the file.
  • Longest edge 2400 px for full view, 800 for cards, 160 for pins.
  • Crop to 4 by 5 for cards with the focal point in the middle third. Keep the original for the full view.
  • Apply the shared grade in CSS only, saturate(0.94) contrast(0.98). It takes the edge off oversharpened phones and turns off in full view.

What to shoot

  • Real moments, unstaged. People in places, not people posing at places.
  • The landmark in the frame is better than the landmark as the subject.
  • Natural light. Flash photos at night are fine, they are honest.
  • Video as short clips, poster frame picked by hand.
  • Never. Screenshots, memes, collages, stickers, text on photos, borders, heavy presets.
